# Value proposition: job lens, canvas, УТП

Used at stage S (drafted), then tested at stage 1 (language), 3-4 (message and price) and 5 (durability, positioning). Everything written at S is a **hypothesis**: Strategyzer's own rule is that a fit is a claim until customers confirm it. Do not present a canvas filled from the founder's head as validated.

Three different things, kept apart:

| | Value proposition (canvas) | УТП (USP) | Positioning |
|---|---|---|---|
| What | the design of the offer matched to jobs, pains, gains | one customer-facing claim | the context that tells buyers what this is, for whom, why it matters |
| Language | structured | the customer's, headline-level | internal, then summarised in one sentence |
| Written | S (hypothesis), refined after interviews | draft at S, tested at 3-4 | provisional sentence at S6, final at stage 5 (`competition.md`) |

## 1. Job lens (JTBD)

- A product is "hired" for a job and "fired" if it does it badly (as paraphrased in search excerpts of Christensen et al., HBR 2016, which is paywalled). The premise visible on the page: innovation stays hit-or-miss because firms profile customers instead of the job.
- **Rule: write the job without naming the product.** Format and wording rules: `discovery.md`, Definitions (the job story, "When..., I want to..., so I can...").
- At S write 1-3 job statements and mark them hypotheses. At stage 1 replace them with the respondents' own words.
- A variant that cannot be stated as a job a real person has is dropped at S4.
- Interview technique (switch interview, timeline, four forces): `discovery.md`.

## 2. Value Proposition Canvas (Osterwalder, Pigneur et al., 2014)

**Customer profile** (one canvas per segment): customer **jobs**, **pains** (obstacles, frustrations, risks), **gains** (outcomes and benefits wanted). Functional / social / emotional job dimensions are commonly cited but not verified in the primary text.
**Value map**: **products and services**, **pain relievers**, **gain creators**.

### Procedure
1. One segment per canvas; with two segments make two canvases.
2. Fill the profile first (jobs, pains, gains), in the customer's words. Features do not belong in the profile.
3. Rank each list by intensity and frequency. Work on the top three of each (our working number; Strategyzer says "focus on the most significant").
4. Fill the value map for the top items only.
5. Mark the **source** of every entry: founder belief / interview n / data. At S most are beliefs.
6. **Coverage check** (at S; not a fit claim): each top pain has a pain reliever and each top gain a gain creator, or is consciously out of scope.

### Three fits and the evidence each needs (Strategyzer, 2014)

| Fit | Evidence required | Our stage |
|---|---|---|
| *none yet* | canvas filled from beliefs | S |
| Problem-solution | customers confirm they care about these jobs, pains and gains; no evidence yet that they would buy | 1-2 |
| Product-market | the proposition relieves pains and delivers gains in practice; assumptions tested repeatedly; traction | 3-4, 8-9 |
| Business-model | the proposition sits inside a profitable, scalable model | 5, 9 |

An earlier Strategyzer article (2012) treats problem-solution and product-market fit as one goal; the 2014 model separates them. This skill uses the 2014 split.

### Template (fields)
Profile: Job | Pain | Gain, each with rank and source. Map: Product/service | Reliever (for which pain) | Creator (for which gain). Coverage: top item -> map item -> evidence -> status (open / confirmed / refuted).

## 3. УТП (USP)

**Origin.** Rosser Reeves, *Reality in Advertising* (1961; the book was not opened, two commentaries were). Three requirements as paraphrased there: (1) a **concrete** offer: a specific benefit the buyer gets, stated in the headline; (2) it is **unique**: competitors cannot or do not make the same offer; (3) it has **pulling power**: strong enough to attract many new buyers. None of the sources checked gives a fill-in template; templates online are derived.

### Three-question check
1. **Concrete.** The benefit is a number, a time or a verifiable result (a dated schedule counts). "Convenient and fast" fails.
2. **Unique** (y / n / unknown). A competitor, or "doing nothing", cannot or does not offer it. Quick heuristic, the **swap test**: put a rival's name in the sentence; if it is still true, it is not yet a УТП. The test appears in later marketing guides with no attribution to Reeves, and as paraphrased his wording asks whether rivals *offer* the benefit, not whether they *could repeat* the words, so the swap test can be stricter: use it as a warning, not a verdict. At S the alternatives are the founder's view and "unique" is often "unknown"; stages 1 and 5 supply the evidence.
3. **Pulls.** A meaningful number of target customers would switch or pay for it. At S this is a hypothesis and goes into the test list; payment or a signed intent confirms it (evidence ladder).

### Format
Headline (up to 12 words, our rule of thumb) + proof (why believable) + what it replaces (the alternative it beats). Illustration only: weak "quality goods for pets" -> stronger "Your kitten's first year: two boxes, one dated checklist".

### Where it is tested
| Stage | Test |
|---|---|
| 1 | does the customer name the pain in the same words? if not, rewrite from their words |
| 3-4 | the headline is the first line of the landing page or pitch; measure requests and payments, not likes (`experiments.md`) |
| 5 | durability and positioning: can a funded competitor say the same within a quarter? (`competition.md`) |

### Positioning (the neighbour)
April Dunford's order, as summarised in a repost of her quickstart: competitive alternatives -> unique attributes -> value -> target customers -> market category. The one-sentence statement (format in `competition.md`) records that work and does not replace it; fill-in-the-blank templates hide choices and give a false sense of being finished. Geoffrey Moore's well-known template has no verified origin in his book. Positioning starts from real alternatives, which need market evidence, so at S the sentence is provisional and is finalised at stage 5. If the УТП and the positioning conflict, rewrite the УТП from the alternatives and the customers' words.

### Anti-patterns
A slogan instead of a benefit · uniqueness that is only a feature rivals copy in a month · a УТП written for ads rather than extracted from interviews · "phantom competitors" customers never see · treating the swap test as proof of demand · listing the УТП as validated before any payment or commitment.
